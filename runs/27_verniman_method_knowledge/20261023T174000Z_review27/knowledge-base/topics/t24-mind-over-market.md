## Teaching selected by Vern

The post endorses an approximately hour-long Mark Douglas interview and his probability-based approach to trading psychology. It treats individual outcomes as uncertain members of a series: an edge describes a tendency, not knowledge of the next result. The article argues that continually adding analysis cannot by itself remove fear of loss or being wrong. A prepared edge, risk controls and acceptance of uncertainty support consistent execution.

Its example of twelve winners and eight losers in twenty trades illustrates an unknown ordering of outcomes. It is not a measured Vern win rate, a guaranteed sequence, or evidence that twenty trades reliably identify an edge.

## Attribution and application

This is Vern's endorsement and summary of Douglas, rather than a transcript of the interview. The singular page title concerns psychology. It must not be confused with James Dalton's book *Mind Over Markets*, which concerns auction/profile understanding.

Abacus interpretation: preparation and execution answer different questions. Analysis determines whether the planned opportunity exists; accepting uncertainty permits acting without demanding certainty. The article's exhortation to execute a pattern presupposes an established edge and controls. It does not demonstrate that any particular pattern has one.
