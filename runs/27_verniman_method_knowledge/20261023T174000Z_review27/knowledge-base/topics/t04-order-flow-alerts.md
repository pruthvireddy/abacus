# T04 — Order Flow Alerts

## What the page actually teaches

**Source statement.** The page presents sound as a second way to notice price, volume and buying/selling activity. It argues that audible feedback can make behavioral patterns easier to recognize while reducing continuous visual attention. TickStrike and FinancialJuice are named as delivery tools. Its proposed use is to draw attention to conditions already specified in a trading plan, rather than to replace that plan.

**Attribution.** This is blog-hosted material. The opening paragraphs closely match PriceSquawk's [product background](https://pricesquawk.com/reviews/), although this article promotes TickStrike. Preserve that vendor lineage; do not turn the performance claim into independently demonstrated evidence about Vern's trading.

## Practical interpretation

**Abacus synthesis.** An alert is an attention mechanism. A useful sequence is: define the market condition worth noticing, encode an observable part of it as a sound, then inspect whether price behavior supports the planned action. A loud buying burst alone does not supply a level, entry, stop or target. Record which information was audible and which decision still required visual/contextual judgment.

The article gives no audio settings, volume threshold, minimum duration, false-alert treatment or product-specific calibration. Its assertions about improved performance and professional adoption remain attributed claims. Do not infer a tested auditory advantage.
