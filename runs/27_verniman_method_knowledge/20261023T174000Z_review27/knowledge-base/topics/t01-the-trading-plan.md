# T01 — The Trading Plan

Source: [The Trading Plan](https://verniman.blogspot.com/2009/09/fed-decision.html),
article reviewed 2026-10-23. No embedded video or article image.

## What Vern explicitly commits to

His plan starts with the larger auction range, then overnight/Globex bias and
the previous session. He selects a bull/bear dividing reference (LIS) and
destinations before looking for a profile-based trade. The preferred entry is
near the location that would disprove the idea. He requires a destination and
cuts exposure when observed pressure no longer supports the trade.

His stated S&P-futures windows are 09:30–11:30 and 15:00–16:00 Eastern; a good
morning can make the afternoon unnecessary. He describes roughly three or four
morning trades, sometimes none. Three consecutive losses end his trading day;
exceeding $500 loss per contract causes a break. These are his historical
personal constraints, not suitable sizing instructions for every account.

He rejects fading a sustained directional move, guessing the day's extreme,
and chasing a missed entry. His approximately 60% win-rate assertion and
roughly two-to-one reward/risk language have no supporting trade ledger here.

## Abacus interpretation

The useful sequence is context → destination → invalidation → feasible entry →
live pressure → management. A price at a marked level is only a location;
it is not sufficient permission to trade. A written plan allows a later
comparison between intended and actual decisions.
