# T27 — Rollover Guide

Source: [CME Rollover Guide](https://verniman.blogspot.com/2014/03/futures-rollover.html). Article text reviewed on 2026-10-23; one embedded video inventoried.

## Vern's operational points

The article describes quarterly index-futures months H/M/U/Z, replacing an expiring position with the next contract, accounting for the price spread, and replacing contract-specific outstanding orders. It distinguishes a spread transaction from executing the legs separately. Its strongest link to his method is liquidity: order-flow reading should follow the contract where volume concentrates; uncertainty around migration can justify standing aside.

The article also discusses first-notice and last-trading dates across futures. Product-specific delivery considerations must not be transferred indiscriminately to ES.

## Historical convention versus current exchange reference

The blog gives the second Thursday of the expiration month as rollover. That is a captured source convention, not a current exchange requirement. The [CME Equity Index Roll Dates page](https://www.cmegroup.com/trading/equity-index/rolldates.html), checked on 2026-10-23, states that participants may roll at any time and gives the Monday before the third Friday as the customary US equity-index roll date. Its December 2026 table lists December 14, with expiration December 18.

The distinction preserves Vern's teaching while preventing the knowledge base from turning an old calendar shorthand into a current mandatory date.

## Research implications

Abacus interpretation: a continuous chart and an executable contract answer different questions. Contract identity and adjustment rules must be explicit before using historical levels or order-flow observations. A rollover spread can change a displayed price without representing the same movement within one contract.
