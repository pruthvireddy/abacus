# T06 — About Statistics

Source: [About Statistics](https://verniman.blogspot.com/2015/05/closing-0514.html). Article text reviewed on 2026-10-23. One embedded video inventoried; transcript not reviewed in this note.

## Vern's use of statistics

He uses precedent to form conditional expectations, then asks present action to confirm or disprove them. Balance/low momentum suggests returning to value; imbalance/high momentum suggests seeking a balancing target. Statistics supply hypotheses, not unconditional entries. The Moneyball analogy concerns maintaining a tested process through a losing sequence.

## Research qualification

The page supplies no dataset, period, code, sample counts, denominators, costs, uncertainty intervals or validation split. The two 78% statements describe different events and must remain separate. Its two 86% statements also differ in wording and conditioning; touch and break are not automatically the same event. Touching one prior boundary does not logically establish an outside day, despite the article's wording. Direction relative to which opening/closing price also requires definition.

Its day-type labels are unusual: the source associates normal with bimodal/trimodal profiles, trend with multimodal profiles, and neutral with remaining inside the overnight range. Preserve those as this source's descriptions. The 72/18/10 figures cannot silently inherit a standard Dalton day-type taxonomy.

Use these as a hypothesis inventory. They cannot establish current probabilities, attainable returns or trade expectancy.
