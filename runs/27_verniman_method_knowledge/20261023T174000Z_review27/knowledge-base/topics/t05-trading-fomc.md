# T05 — Trading FOMC

The article's ABCD chart was actually inspected; see [chart case C01](../chart-cases.md#c01--fomc-impulse-retracement-destination) for visible marks, interpretation and limits. The other article image is a meeting photograph, not market evidence.

Source: [Trading FOMC](https://verniman.blogspot.com/2014/03/trading-fomc.html). Article text reviewed on 2026-10-23. No article-body chart or video was visible in the retrieved page.

## What Vern explicitly describes

He proposes waiting through the announcement's initial impulse, AB, and its counter-move, BC. BC should recover at least half of AB. The trade seeks the next leg in AB's direction, using C's confluence with a profile reference: VAH, VAL, HOD, LOD, POC or HVE. The article's example uses a five-point stop, takes half off after five points, moves the remaining stop to entry, and trails toward an AB–CD completion. An alternative starts with half size and adds when a subsequent bar exceeds the entry bar's high. He explicitly makes context a condition, rather than claiming a universal news rule.

## Interpretation and unresolved definitions

The sequence distinguishes a forecast from permission to enter: his claim about the first impulse does not authorize trading that impulse. Confluence and waiting are central.

The page leaves several decisions undefined: which bar interval, how AB ends, when a retracement invalidates the sequence, how to define a level cluster, and how to trail. The high-based add description is not separately explained for shorts. Treat five points as the source's historical example, not a volatility-independent prescription. No sample or performance test accompanies the pattern.

## Calendar boundary

The [Federal Reserve calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm) currently states eight regular annual meetings, with others as needed. Current release timing must come from the event's official schedule. This verifies scheduling context only; it does not verify Vern's price-path hypothesis.
