## The selected developmental model

**Source statement.** The page contrasts receiving a ready-made strategy with learning foundations, practicing, and reviewing one's behavior. Money management, organization, market structure, value and order-flow interpretation are named as parts of development. Exercises are presented as a means of discovering strengths and weaknesses and building an individual edge.

**Attribution.** The article explicitly credits PriceSquawk. Its reference to Mark Douglas is PriceSquawk's characterization of Douglas's lesson, rather than a quoted rule verified against the original book. Claims about typical prop training remain attributed descriptions.

## Useful practical consequence

**Abacus synthesis.** Define the skill an exercise isolates before judging the outcome: noticing a change in flow, entering without hesitation under a rule, accepting a predefined loss, or reviewing inconsistent decisions. Record behavior and exceptions as well as P&L. An exercise can improve compliance or perception while supplying no evidence of a profitable live strategy.

Keep the exercise's constraints distinct from recommendations for normal trading. A drill may intentionally remove context or force exposure to demonstrate a behavior. Do not turn such a constraint into Vern's universal trading rule. Any new Abacus drill is a proposed learning aid, clearly separate from the historical source.
