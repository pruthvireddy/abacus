## Part 1 — Profile as a hypothesis about commitment

[Davies/Jigsaw, 16 January 2014](https://www.jigsawtrading.com/blog/video-day-trading-without-charts-part-1/) describes the intraday profile as traded quantity per price. It uses that history to form educated guesses about whether traders remain involved, which areas a winning side may defend, where stops might lie and where price may travel quickly or slowly. These are behavioral hypotheses, rather than holdings disclosed by the profile. Their purpose is to identify favorable location and a defined pain point. The companion's claim about prop trainees reaching profitability quickly is not supported by a study on that page.

## Part 2 — Commitment, patience and missed fills

[Davies/Jigsaw, 17 January 2014](https://www.jigsawtrading.com/blog/video-day-trading-without-charts-part-2/) explains how a move away from a high-volume area can change the behavior of traders who lost, those waiting to exit and those holding winners. It proposes that small high-volume nodes can develop into broader ranges, so suspected ranges deserve time. Reversals need not be entered at the extreme; a hold can provide additional evidence. Order flow and related markets are named as confirmation.

Its example watches offers above a desired buy limit shrink and may cross rather than wait for a nonfill. Otherwise it warns against chasing a missed trade, with a specific momentum exception in the example. This is a conditional execution tradeoff, not permission to chase all signals. Tick distances are expressly based on ES and require adjustment for other liquidity conditions.

## A live discussion — Do not misidentify the participant

[The Jigsaw chat example, 5 December 2013](https://www.jigsawtrading.com/blog/trading-without-charts-live-volume-profile-analysis/) records Peter Davies coaching a participant named **Vitaly**, whose initials are VM. VM must not be identified as Vern. The discussion uses a developing profile, possible rotation zones, edge responses and acceleration after a break. Davies says the precise boundary selection is discretionary, and cautions that a larger key level could change the directional interpretation. A return into a former accepted area may churn; it does not automatically force an immediate stop-out of the new side.

The page contains a later reported gain and participant interpretations of motives, neither independently verified. Its chat-referenced pictures were not visually inspected in this branch, so this is textual reasoning rather than chart confirmation.

Web screenshot retrieval failed, and the local-download attempt was aborted; no slide-chart inspection is claimed here. A separate blog-hosted slide actually inspected by the project is in [chart case C07](chart-cases.md#c07--the-chartless-trading-slide).

**Abacus synthesis.** The usable abstraction is conditional: locate prior trading interest, hypothesize a response, wait for evidence appropriate to the setup, and accept the possibility of no fill. The method supplies reasons to watch a level; it does not transform volume history into exact knowledge of everybody's positions.
