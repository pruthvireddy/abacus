# The connected model

This is **Abacus synthesis** of the selected articles and reviewed media.
It organizes the teaching; it is not a new statement attributed verbatim to Vern
and is not an executable or tested trading system.

## 1. Start with the auction's problem

The starting question is where participants have accepted trade, and whether
the market is rotating around that area or discovering a different one. Price
explores; sustained activity develops value. This makes a move away from a POC
ambiguous: it may fail and return, or build new participation and shift value.
The distinction determines whether a fade or a continuation idea even belongs
in the scenario set. See [Value Transition](topics/t16-value-transition.md) and
[Trading Value](topics/t18-trading-value.md).

In Vern's stated plan, the larger auction and overnight context come before a local ladder signal.
A daily HVN, an overnight extreme and a developing intraday node answer
different questions. Label their source sessions and horizons rather than
treating every horizontal mark as interchangeable. This is not a universal
requirement of every curated method: Davies's seconds-long spread-capture
examples can omit larger profile context. That is a different game and
preparatory policy, as the [scalping comparison](video-scalping.md) explains.

## 2. Give every level a role

Vern's map contains destinations, candidate entry locations and dividing
references. HVNs/POCs describe accepted activity; LVNs and volume edges describe
boundaries or thin areas. LIS expresses the scenario split. PBT describes a
projected balancing destination. An overnight high can be a test location
without becoming either a guaranteed target or a mandatory reversal.

This separates three decisions: **where to investigate**, **what to expect
there**, and **what observation would justify acting**. A level is useful
because of its relationship to value and a scenario, not because it is a line.
See [level selection and drawing limits](levels.md).

## 3. Read response rather than worship the mark

At a candidate location, look at transactions and the price response, with
displayed liquidity as a changing context. Large aggressive buying with little
upward progress can suggest absorption; large buying with sustained progress
can mean something different. The observation needs both activity and response.
Neither a large print nor a bright heatmap band identifies an institution's
intent. A static profile also does not reveal who still holds a position.

The linked teachers illustrate several lenses: Bookmap's historical depth,
DOM/time-and-sales, audio activity alerts, and correlated-market confirmation.
They are curated tools with different authors and assumptions. The source
research does not establish one required software stack or a proprietary
Vern indicator. See the [Bookmap course](bookmap-course.md) and
[order-flow review](video-order-flow-review.md).

## 4. Express a trade as a falsifiable proposition

A useful narrative has an expected destination and a structural point where
the expectation stops making sense. Vern favors a good entry near that
invalidation, avoiding a chase that worsens the remaining opportunity. His
instruction to cut when pressure changes adds a live response condition; it
does not specify a numerical pressure threshold.

Structural logic and account risk must both fit. A deliberately tiny money
stop can be inside ordinary movement, while an appropriate structural stop
may make a proposed size impossible. The curated FT71 material discusses
sizing, scaling and distributions; its examples are not Vern's personal rules.
See [risk and management](video-ft71-risk-management.md).

## 5. Keep the state allowed to change

Balance-fading depends on balance continuing. When new acceptance develops,
the old center may cease to be the relevant destination. Conversely, an
apparent breakout that fails to attract participation can restore a rotation
scenario. Historical examples do not authorize labeling every pause as
reversal or every POC uptick as trend. Compare the
[VPOC lessons](video-value-transition.md),
[Mitchell's auction lecture](video-auction-mitchell.md), and
[Dalton](topics/t19-dalton-unplugged.md).

## 6. Separate evidence, expectancy and emotional discipline

Vern's statistics are contextual hypotheses with missing data definitions.
They cannot substitute for a feasible entry/exit model or a calculation of
net expectancy. FT71's statistical process offers research methods, while
Douglas's probability language addresses how a trader behaves when outcomes
remain uncertain. A psychological conviction cannot establish an edge.

Patience, missed-trade acceptance and a limited trade budget protect the
decision process. They do not eliminate loss or make a weak hypothesis true.
Use [the statistics audit](topics/t06-about-statistics.md),
[FT71 research process](video-ft71-statistics.md), and
[unresolved questions](unknowns-and-tensions.md).

## A compact state map

| Observed context | Candidate question | What would change the idea? |
|---|---|---|
| Existing balance; price near an edge | Does rejection support a return toward accepted trade? | New sustained activity outside the old area |
| Directional exploration away from old value | Is participation building a new value area? | Failed progress and return to the old distribution |
| Pullback after new participation | Does a contextual boundary still hold? | Contrary trade sustained through the structural reference |
| News impulse and retracement | Does the contextual third-leg scenario form? | Missing retracement/location or invalidation; not merely an announcement |
| Choppy or contradictory observations | Is there a coherent location, destination and risk proposition at all? | Clarity may return; standing aside is a valid state |

This table is an organizing aid. Exact observation windows, persistence tests
and entry thresholds remain undeclared until a future empirical protocol.
