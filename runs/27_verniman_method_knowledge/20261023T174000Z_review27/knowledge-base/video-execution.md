# Execution videos: what changes at the location?

All four exported English auto-caption transcripts linked by
[Trading Execution](topics/t07-trading-execution.md) were read in full.
Timestamp links below identify the reviewed passages. This is transcript
analysis; no video frames or audio were independently inspected. Numerical
examples and speaker identities not established in the captions remain qualified.

## Tape Reading ThinkorSwim — activity builds a working reference

[00:00–07:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=laX4eNcM85U).
The instructor treats a burst of prints near a price as a provisional reference
and then wants activity to develop above it. As the move progresses, the
working reference updates; an old level is not necessarily the current risk
reference. Around [06:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=laX4eNcM85U&t=393s),
a tiny trade below the level is explicitly distinguished from meaningful
failure. This is evidence against turning every one-tick touch into automatic
invalidation. The captioned 100–200 lot sizes are local illustrations, not
portable ES thresholds. Price formatting is ambiguous; do not reconstruct an
exact tick-by-tick trade ledger from it.

## Jigsaw tape lesson — confirmation has a price

[00:00–08:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B3UYyrXEqzo).
At [00:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B3UYyrXEqzo&t=11s), the lesson gives
a new tape reader one directional premise to test. If the specified evidence
does not appear, pass; it does not mean take the opposite trade. Around
[03:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B3UYyrXEqzo&t=185s), heavy selling
without downward progress is interpreted as passive buyers absorbing it.
Later, buying becomes more visibly effective. The later entry has more
confirmation but a worse price and less room to a nearby high
([05:43–07:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B3UYyrXEqzo&t=343s)).

The practical teaching is to define the evidence needed at a location while
accounting for its cost in price. It is not a license to anticipate any reversal.
The transcript's verbal delta definition and the sign examples are inconsistent;
verify the platform convention rather than adopt its spoken subtraction order.

## FT71 absorption demonstration — context is essential, and the demo loses

[00:01–08:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WsVP-c3cwrc).
FT71 uses recorded playback and repeatedly says he has no actual homework
level for the chosen demonstration. His live-trade practice is to stalk a
preselected area; this demonstration deliberately does not establish such a
location. He looks for offers to replenish as buying executes without gaining
ground. Buyers and sellers still match in executed volume; aggression differs
([03:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WsVP-c3cwrc&t=186s)).

The example enters and is stopped near [08:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WsVP-c3cwrc&t=485s).
Retaining that loss matters: perceived absorption is fallible, and the video
does not demonstrate a validated setup. The temporary five-tick stop and
multiple target brackets are demonstration settings, not a recovered Vern rule.

## Daily Cycles — expected follow-through depends on current activity

[00:00–08:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3JhAcw8qP1o).
The speaker offers four illustrative states rather than an exhaustive taxonomy:
active participation around pressure points; slower but still methodical
movement; movement that entices entries without useful follow-through; and
near inactivity. Management should fit the state. A slower move can require
patience and a predefined loss rather than instant progress; an inactive
market may justify stopping. See
[03:38–04:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3JhAcw8qP1o&t=218s) and
[06:32–08:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3JhAcw8qP1o&t=392s).

Claims about which participant group causes a state are the speaker's
interpretations. The video does not empirically establish them, estimate odds,
or make all slow conditions identical. Its labels help observation; they do
not supply a mechanical classifier.
