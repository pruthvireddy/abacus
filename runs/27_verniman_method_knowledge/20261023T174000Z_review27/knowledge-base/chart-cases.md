# Reading the source charts

These observations come from **actual visual inspection of the original linked
image bytes**, at the largest resolution linked by each article. Six market
charts and one instructional slide were inspected, along with four decorative
images. The five institutional-office pictures on T26 were inventoried but
not visually reviewed. Retained image locators/hashes are in the run's image
inventory. Links below lead to the original blog context; the knowledge base
does not reproduce the source images.

Each case separates visible marks from an interpretation. No image supplies
order-by-order replay, independent trade outcomes or proof that a mark was
available before the pictured move. A filename date is not an independently
verified market timestamp.

## C01 — FOMC: impulse, retracement, destination

Source: [Trading FOMC](https://verniman.blogspot.com/2014/03/trading-fomc.html),
second image, retained as `T05-image-2.png` (602 × 471).

**Visible:** A dark chart labeled FOMC ABCD Pattern marks A near the lower end
of a price impulse, B near 3960, C near 3944, and D around the horizontal
3974.5 reference. The C area contains a buy annotation. A green PBT magnet is
marked at 3980; HVE references appear at 3960 and 3936. A blue dashed path
illustrates the impulse, retracement and renewed rise.

**Reading:** C locates the conditional continuation idea after a substantial
retracement; D and the PBT are destination references. It is a context-plus-
location illustration, not permission to buy the initial announcement spike.

**Limit:** Exact bar duration, fills, stop execution and whether the targets
were selected beforehand cannot be recovered. The pictured path alone does
not validate a fixed five-point stop or a general FOMC success rate. The first
article image is a meeting photograph, not a market chart.

## C02 — Execution: the location and the tape answer different questions

Source: [Trading Execution](https://verniman.blogspot.com/2013/09/trading-execution_8.html),
`T07-image-1.png` (451 × 397).

**Visible:** The left pane labels Bullish Imbalance and HVE levels at 4108,
4119 and 4135, with a buy annotation near the lower local profile structure.
The right time-and-sales pane highlights multiple prints at 4125.00, including
sizes 200 and 335. Prices, a profile and executed activity share the image.

**Reading:** Profile structure locates the scenario; transaction activity
illustrates a separate layer of response/confirmation. A conspicuous print
does not itself select the auction destination.

**Limit:** We cannot reconstruct the sequence or aggressor-classification
method from a still frame. Highlighted sizes are examples, not a universal
large-trade threshold. The green chart label is not proof of the actual fill.

## C03 — Excess: the author's annotation needs a measurement definition

Source: [Market Excess](https://verniman.blogspot.com/2013/04/the-profile-traders_26.html),
`T15-image-1.png` (662 × 532).

**Visible:** An elongated red oval labeled Excess covers a thinner upper
section above a fuller lower distribution. The image also labels GAP 2549.75,
GAP 2536, Edge 2525, GAP 2516.75, LVN 2510 and CLVN 2497.

**Reading:** The annotation draws attention to low participation above a
previously fuller acceptance area. It shows that Vern's map contains several
different reference types rather than a single support/resistance category.

**Limit:** A thin volume tail is not direct evidence of TPO single prints.
The article discusses a single-print tail, but the shown volume distribution
does not establish a numerical excess rule, participant identity or automatic
reversal. CLVN is visible but not defined in the selected glossary; do not
silently expand it as a confirmed Vern definition.

## C04 — Balance and an upper low-volume extension

Source: same article, `T15-image-2.png` (659 × 544).

**Visible:** A wide lower distribution is circled and labeled Balance, roughly
between the low 350s and 380. A much narrower upper extension reaches the 417.25
marker and is circled beside Excess = Lack of Volume; Out of Balance is printed
between the two regions.

**Reading:** The contrast between the broad lower acceptance and thin upper
exploration is the teaching object. The interpretation depends on the reference
distribution rather than only the latest high.

**Limit:** The ticker, session aggregation and timing are not legible/declared
in the image. Do not assume it is ES just because the blog's main focus is ES.
The profile shape cannot prove who bought or whether an auction is complete.

## C05 — LVN rejection and conditional paths

Source: [Trading Value](https://verniman.blogspot.com/2013/03/trade-market-value.html),
`T18-image-2.png` (752 × 396).

**Visible:** A yellow Key Level box is near 1765–1765.75; the ladder highlights
the same thin area and labels LVN=Rejection. Two large arrows show conditional
directions. Other marked references include 1762.75, 1770.5, 1775 and 1755.

**Reading:** The thin area is a decision boundary. The two arrows communicate
alternatives around structure rather than a claim that the nearest line always
bounces. This supports distinguishing scenario, entry location and destination.

**Limit:** The still does not establish the threshold for rejecting or accepting
that band, which arrow was forecast first, or a completed trade result.

## C06 — Low volume entry reference versus accepted trade

Source: same article, `T18-image-3.png` (742 × 373).

**Visible:** The left pane labels LVN 1763 Support. On the right, a lower yellow
box labels LVN around the 1763 area, while an upper box labels Value around
1764.5. Horizontal chart references include 1770, 1764.5 and 1759. The volume
bars differ strongly across adjacent prices.

**Reading:** The image concretely distinguishes a candidate support/entry
reference from an accepted activity/destination area. A new reader should be
able to explain why those are different roles before using either one.

**Limit:** One frame cannot establish whether value was developing or final,
which trades actually filled, or whether the next move followed the annotation.

## C07 — The chartless-trading slide

Source: [Try without charts](https://verniman.blogspot.com/2014/01/trade-without-charts.html),
`T12-image-1.png` (700 × 450), associated with Peter Davies/Jigsaw's lesson.

**Visible teaching:** Give a suspected range time to develop; consider the
history before a node formed and its distance from a fuller volume area;
consider several node outcomes; use order-flow/intermarket observations for
confirmation; adapt to thinner/thicker instruments. The slide describes
reversal as the least likely of its node scenarios, without supplying a sample.

**Reading:** Chartless means a different representation of market activity,
not a context-free or risk-free process. The slide's principles belong to
the curated Davies material; they are not evidence of Vern's exact entries.

The other inspected images are a glossary banner, book covers, an auction-room
photograph and the FOMC meeting photograph. They are not market evidence.
